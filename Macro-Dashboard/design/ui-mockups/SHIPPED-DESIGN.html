The Secured Overnight Financing Rate is what banks pay for repo collateralized by Treasuries — the post-LIBOR risk-free benchmark. When SOFR spikes above the Fed's IORB rate, it signals the repo plumbing is breaking and dealers can't fund their balance sheets. September 2019's 525bp blowout forced the Fed to restart QE within weeks.

Watch sustained moves above IORB+10bp as a five-alarm fire for funding markets. Currently sitting calmly 1bp above IORB — system functioning normally.

+12bp 5d · elevated
Level 3 of 5Elevated
via FRED

The 10-year Treasury yield is the benchmark long-term rate that prices everything from mortgages to discount cash flows. The level matters less than the velocity — fast moves break things in markets that rely on stable duration assumptions.

Above 5% historically signals stress, but the more important question is rate of change: a 50bp move in two weeks is more dangerous than 100bp over six months. This is the price of money — the foundation underneath all asset classes.

via FRED

High-yield Option-Adjusted Spread measures the premium investors demand to lend to junk-rated corporations over Treasuries. It's the canonical risk-appetite gauge — when spreads widen, money is fleeing risk; when they tighten, animal spirits are returning.

Above 700bp signals real recession territory; the 2008 GFC peak hit 2000bp. The single most useful credit signal for retail tracking, available free via FRED.

The yen cross is currently the single most important FX pair for global macro because of the yen carry trade — when borrowed yen funds long positions in higher-yielding assets globally, an unwind can crash everything simultaneously.

Above 160 triggers Japanese Ministry of Finance intervention, as seen in April and July 2024. The August 2024 carry-trade unwind correlated with a sharp Bitcoin drawdown.

Overnight Reverse Repo balance is money parked at the Fed when there is nowhere else to deploy it — the system's liquidity buffer. As it drains toward zero, T-bill demand is exhausted and Treasury auctions get harder, forcing yields up.

Below $50 billion is the danger zone where mounting Treasury issuance starts breaking things. Currently sub-$1B — the buffer is essentially gone.

Japan's 10-year government bond yield — the YCC (yield curve control) death watch. Above 2% means the Bank of Japan's losses on existing JGB holdings balloon and the entire JGB market becomes unstable.

This ripples globally because Japanese investors are the marginal buyer of duration everywhere — from US Treasuries to French OATs to Australian bonds. A breakdown here is a true regime change for global fixed income.
